The art of programming is the art of organizing complexity, of mastering multitude and avoiding its bastard chaos as effectively as possible. Edsger Dijkstra wrote this in the early days of structured programming, and it remains the central insight of the discipline. Every system we build is, at its heart, a system for managing the entropy that emerges when many simple things interact in many simple ways. Languages, frameworks, design patterns, paradigms — they all exist to give us leverage against the combinatorial explosion of possibility. The most elegant code is not the cleverest; it is the most boring. It is the code that does exactly what it says, no more, no less, and which can be read by a stranger six months later without surprise. Surprise is the enemy of correctness. Surprise is the enemy of maintainability. Surprise is the enemy of the well-rested engineer at three in the morning when production has caught fire.
